# Coherent explanation and fine body units

Use the [Copy contract](copy-package-contract.md) and
[reader-quality guidance](../../../packages/contracts/reader-quality.md).
First explain the supported story for the reader, then decompose the body as
finely as fluent language and the argument permit. Decomposition changes the
granularity of the handoff, not the amount or strength of the information.

A paragraph can yield multiple body units for distinct facts, explanations,
comparisons, implications or qualifications. Several units may belong to one
heading. Prefer the smallest meaningful passage; no fixed sentence, character
or unit count defines it. Keep a condition with the claim it limits, or retain
an inseparable causal/grammatical construction when splitting would obscure it.
Never turn a clear explanation into noun fragments just to make units smaller.

Retain subjects when needed, punctuation and connecting words. Read the result
continuously against the original explanation. In existing body-relation
purposes, identify what each unit contributes and any material dependency on
another passage; do not write only "explains H1". Map source findings to each
unit's actual content rather than blindly copying a whole page's source list.

A unit identifies approved wording for traceability. Art may combine several
units in one paragraph/text object, present them separately, or emphasize a
part, while preserving meaning and dependencies. Unit boundaries are not
separate-object instructions and do not authorize rewriting the wording.

Title and subtitle are Storylines. Headings have any positive level; body and
annotations have no heading level. Do not emit parent/sibling trees,
render-separately flags, merge bans, media kinds or visual-order constraints.
Art may combine units and freely decide presentation while preserving meaning.

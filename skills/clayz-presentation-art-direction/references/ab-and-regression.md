# A/B Prototyping and Fixed Regression

## When A/B is mandatory

Produce two genuinely different gray-box candidates for any of these conditions:

- a hero, section-transition, decision, or complex relationship/model slide;
- no natural primary evidence or a high risk of cardification;
- adjacent slides have a similar silhouette unless that repetition is required by a meaningful Logic series;
- two admissible reference skeletons imply materially different approaches; or
- the slide is being used to learn an explicit user preference.

## What counts as genuinely different

Candidates must differ in at least two of the following: composition backbone, first visual, dominant medium, area ratio, reading path, or evidence-to-meaning-to-action coupling.

Moving the same shell from left to right, changing color or corner radius, switching three columns to three rows, or replacing icons does not constitute A/B.

Both candidates must preserve the same Logic, approved Copy, theme, and communication task. Review each independently before making a pairwise comparison. `both-insufficient` is a valid intermediate outcome. Scores, saliency, layout metrics, and similarity signals are diagnostic only.

## Render–observe–repair loop

1. Record a semantic-tree signature for each candidate and render a real gray-box prototype.
2. Inspect each candidate separately for production integrity, text readability, attention hierarchy, structural semantics, and composition-to-task fit.
3. Record what to `preserve` and what to `change`.
4. Run no more than two rounds. If both candidates are insufficient, rebuild. If they converge, restore a real structural difference.
5. Select through professional Art Direction judgment and record an explicit stop reason; automated signals never approve a candidate.

The observation loop is informed by the visual-assessment framing in [VASCAR](https://github.com/XinyuanWangCS/VASCAR). That source supports the observation categories and iterative inspection pattern only; this package's contract, authority boundaries, and approval rules remain local.

## Judgment dimensions

Use these dimensions as prompts, not a weighted scorecard:

1. Can the title proposition be recognized within three seconds?
2. Does the dominant area match the slide's decision weight?
3. Does the composition express the intended relation rather than merely place objects?
4. Are evidence, meaning, and action visibly coupled?
5. Is every locked copy unit present and readable at the configured minimum size?
6. Does the slide create an intentional rhythm with its neighbors?
7. Are semantic shapes used without default container stacking?
8. Can Output build the plan as editable objects?
9. Does area allocation reflect semantic load?
10. For a series, are invariants preserved while new information becomes the focus?
11. Is semantic whitespace protected where it carries meaning?
12. Is visual grammar doing the work instead of punctuation-like labels and dividers?
13. Are related data consolidated into a chart or table rather than fragmented into cards?
14. For management recommendations, are the dilemma, success criteria, and trade-off established before the recommendation?

Record independent analyses and the decisive differences. “B looks better” is not an explanation.

## Fixed regression set

Maintain synthetic, organization-neutral fixtures that cover at least:

- four material routes: management report, business analysis, strategy deployment, and sales training;
- four cross-slide cases: stable series, controlled variation, intentional series break, and non-series silhouette collision;
- management-request order and related-data integration; and
- registry lookup, human admission, traceability, neighbor context, and reference-budget behavior.

Regression tests validate selection logic, contracts, and failure behavior. They are not a visual-quality oracle and must contain no private templates or proprietary examples.

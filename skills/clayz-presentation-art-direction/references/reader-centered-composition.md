# Reader-centered composition

Layout organizes attention and understanding. It helps a reader perceive what
matters, which information belongs together, how a relationship works and where
to read next. Comfortable pacing supports sustained attention. Alignment and
legibility provide a foundation; the composition must also do explanatory work.

Use these prompts while designing and reviewing actual pages. They are core Art
guidance, not a learning package, template collection or machine-scored gate.
Art owns the judgments and revisions within the existing content boundaries.

## Start with the reader's task

Read the whole story and identify what this page helps the reader understand.
Find the main judgment, its evidence, the relationships and the necessary
conditions. Decide what should become apparent before the reader works through
every paragraph. The first visual may be a contrast, relationship, trend or
critical condition; choosing the title as the first visual on every page does
not complete this decision.

A textual heading marks meaning. It does not require a separate box, equal
area or the same visual weight as another heading. Choose grouping and emphasis
according to the communication task, while preserving the approved meaning.
Art may clarify supported objects, actions and context within its recorded
presentation projection; a wording change is not automatically a return to
Copy. Do not invent an unsupported relationship. A genuine evidence gap follows
the current workflow authority: when an upstream checkpoint is already closed,
record the remaining limitation for the report or future work rather than
reopening Logic or Copy outside the finite cycle.

## Let space explain relationships

Proximity, separation, alignment, nesting, scale and connections can make a
relationship perceptible. Select the combination that suits this content.
The placement should help readers recognize grouping, comparison, sequence,
conditions or feedback with less reconstruction from prose. An arrangement
must not invent a sequence, cause, rank or numerical proportion.

Distinguish different relationships within a page. A process may have a main
path, conditions attached to particular actions and an accompanying explanation
of revenue. They need not receive the same spatial treatment. Boxes and arrows
are available tools, with meaning determined by their actual use. Avoid choosing
a grid from the number of headings and then finding reasons to justify it.

Several related numbers can share a comparison or other visual encoding when
the approved data supports it. Labels, units and conditions belong close enough
to the evidence to be understood together. A chart that merely repeats all the
prose adds work. Art can adapt supported wording to the selected medium and
record the actual change; this does not authorize new facts. Tabular
presentations retain the native-table requirements.

## Construct a representation from its meaning

Start from the objects and relations the reader needs for this judgment. An
object might be an institution, a contractual role, a dated state, or a measure
with its entity, period and denominator. Keep that identity recognizable when
the same object appears in several parts of the explanation. A shared name or
aligned position can help correspondence; proximity alone does not establish it.

Decide what the visible properties mean. Position might denote chronology,
category membership or a quantitative value; a connection might denote a
permitted movement, a dependency or an association. These are different uses.
Choose labels, direction, reference points and conditions that let the reader
identify the intended use in the actual composition. A line described in work
notes as a time interval does not communicate an interval when neither endpoint
nor the state between them can be recognized in the image.

Combine useful expressions when their meanings fit. Shared axes can support a
comparison when the relevant domains, units and periods are compatible. Aligned
regions can connect different measures of the same named objects without
pretending that their scales are interchangeable. Conditions may attach to a
specific action while a separate explanation shows its revenue role. Reason
from the identities and relationships being composed, rather than selecting
a chart family or filling a predefined diagram. These examples do not prescribe
axes, regions, arrows or any other object on each page.

Judge the combination as well as its parts. A small mark may lose a shape cue;
an overlapping label may obscure the correspondence it was meant to establish;
one shared position may imply a false pairing. Reconsider emphasis, scale,
grouping or separation if the combined result makes the intended relationship
hard to recognize. Where detailed lookup and pattern comparison compete for
space, compare an integrated treatment with adjacent, consistently named views.
Retain the information the audience needs; page count and mark count do not
decide this tradeoff.

Inspect the actual draft using the current understanding task: can the reader
identify the objects, follow the intended relation and distinguish it from
plausible but unsupported meanings? Then read the conditions and check the
underlying values. The representation should express the selected supported
facts without inventing additional ones. It need not display every available
research value on one page. Selection remains governed by the approved story
and required coverage.

When Art adds a page or rewrites a title within its permitted projection,
preserve the original title commitment as well as the data. If the brief asks
for a supported factual judgment, a label describing a lookup method cannot
replace it; place that reading guidance where it explains the evidence. Record
material choices and observed effects in existing Art notes. This method adds
no form, gate, layout quota or claim of measured reading-speed improvement.

## Let a comparison serve the current judgment

Identify the action the comparison supports. Finding a difference, quantifying
it, dissecting a composition and connecting a change to its context require
different reading work. Domain names can conceal the actual targets: two basis
states of one measure are not two years, and returns with different denominators
are not interchangeable amounts. Make targets, reference and scope recognizable
along the selected reading path before choosing which relations to externalize.

Difficulty can arise independently from the number of targets, the complexity
of each target or the complexity of their relationships. Two sets of values
can require several correspondences; many simple targets may support a scan.
Locate the burden in the intended action rather than inferring it from object,
page or chart counts or prescribing an arrangement from those counts.

Juxtaposition retains individual context and leaves some connecting work to the
reader. Superposition can reduce some locating work but requires compatible
references and distinguishable targets. Explicitly encoding a verified
difference, ratio or offset can perform some calculation while hiding original
amounts or explanations. Combine, separate or retain prose as the task warrants,
then inspect whether targets and relations remain recognizable in the actual
image. These are tradeoffs, not three required templates or a ranking.

Summarizing targets before comparing them and summarizing their local relations
after comparison can answer different questions. Similar totals do not establish
similar components; a net change does not show its distribution. Preserve a
clear lookup path for approved detail and avoid turning a selected relation into
unsupported complete attribution. A changed reference or emphasis also changes
the question made visible; assess that effect with the necessary conditions.

Read one actual comparison through the intended action: identify its targets
and reference, then recognize, compute or connect the required relation and
retain the context that still needs explanation. Observe correspondence,
labels and conditions. Complete text, the existence of a chart or a correct
retelling does not establish faster comparison. Record material choices in the
existing Art notes, without new forms, gates, template menus or count quotas.

## Keep visible grouping consistent with actual ownership

Distinguish content ownership from the grouping a viewer could perceive. A native group, shared type style or common area does not establish which label, value or qualification belongs to which object. Trace a correspondence in the current page: what does the label name, does the qualification constrain one object or several, and are the objects merely compared or actually nested?

Proximity, similar treatment, common regions and connections can suggest membership, and their suggestions may differ. Consistent treatment can identify comparable quantities while a local label still appears attached to the wrong mark. A common period can support state correspondence; nested products need their inclusion scope. A shared area or adjacent position does not establish exclusive membership, causality or a common denominator. Consider separation, alignment, explicit names or necessary connections from the current meaning, without prescribing frames, chart types or a universal relationship diagram.

Inspect the actual whole page and its neighbors to see whether local correspondence remains recognizable in the composition. Ordinary text capacity measurements and native inventories may omit chart-internal labels; a pass in one scope does not approve an unmeasured scope. Revise the observed interference while preserving values, scope and explanation, then observe the affected pages. Use existing Art records and original reader and repair allowances; do not add grouping forms, automatic scores or layout quotas.

## Check the question the visible representation answers

Identify the supported judgment, its population and period, and the relation the reader needs to understand. Then read the dominant representation for the comparison it actually invites: which objects were selected, and which judgment becomes easier through position, scale, grouping or change marks? Attention hierarchy determines where the reader looks first; representation choices also determine which question can be answered there. A conclusion title alone cannot supply a different relation missing from that view.

A population’s trend over time and its current difference from a subset are different reading tasks, as are a stock composition and a period’s net change. Placing accounting amounts together does not automatically express the operations or ownership transitions. Decide from the current content which relations the main expression should carry directly and which series support further inspection. These examples identify meaning; they are not a chart menu, pagination rule or requirement to replace useful prose with graphics.

When the main view invites a different judgment, reconsider the selected objects, scale, composition, placement and necessary names. Bolding the title or removing relevant counterevidence does not resolve the difference. Complete series can cooperate with a local comparison while preserving population, period, inclusion and unexplained scope. Use only approved evidence within the actual repair allowance; return new research needs to the responsible stage.

Inspect the real page again for the primary comparison together with its necessary qualifications, then verify values and sources. Numerical accuracy, transparent provenance and capacity checks have distinct scopes. None alone establishes that the chosen visual question serves the supported judgment or improves reader speed. Use existing Art records and reader workflows without new scores, gates or form quotas.

## Anchor marks to their actual references

Identify the function of each graphic object and the reference it depends on. A naming label refers to an object or dimension, a connector relates objects, and a quantitative bar depends on its baseline and scale. These anchors differ. A rule change between years belongs at the relevant column boundary; a metric label must retain the population, period and scope needed for the reader’s judgment. When shortening a name, inspect the actual image to confirm that the same referent remains identifiable. An abbreviation, proximity or matching color does not establish that correspondence. Use the current reader task; do not require every short name to expand or every page to acquire a standard note.

When implementing a mark or connector next to a table, derive its reference from the returned or reopened native table’s actual origin, column widths and row heights. A planned equal division may no longer apply after content layout. The optional packages.layout.semantic_table.table_grid_boundary helper reads a current top-level, unrotated and unflipped native table grid and returns integer EMU endpoints. The author still selects a justified row or column boundary and decides what the mark means. The helper neither identifies years, infers relationships nor changes a table, and an internal grid boundary is not necessarily a visible border across merged cells. Handle unsupported transformations explicitly.

In the actual render, trace labels and marks to their objects and references, then check original quantities, scales and conditions. Correct grid coordinates establish implementation placement, not the selected year, statistical scope or relation; approved content does not establish visible correspondence either. Local changes follow existing ownership and bounded reader cycles. Use existing Art records without line, figure, word or page quotas, and do not infer faster reading from one successful restatement.

## Carry identity across changes of representation

An object may do different explanatory work in a plot, a table, continuous prose, or adjacent pages. Identify the entity, period, or scope that must persist, and whether the transition changes scale, grouping, or argumentative role. Identity can persist while position changes. Matching colors, ordering, or correct values alone do not establish correspondence.

Make the change understandable. When a plot and table use compatible annual references, their actual marks, years, and values can correspond. Independent references can instead use clear domains, names, or necessary connections. When moving from a result to its explanation, show whether the next view develops that result, supplies partial evidence, or leaves a difference unexplained. Sequence must not imply a complete causal account. Choose for the meaning and use; shared axes, a fixed layout, and animation are not requirements.

Before implementation, distinguish marks carrying data or relations from objects used for separation, hierarchy, or decoration. Similar stroke widths, colors, or shapes do not establish similar responsibilities. A change to annual correspondence should not select a title separator solely by its appearance. Geometry tools can read the current carrier; the author still chooses the objects that meaningfully depend on it.

Read the transition in the actual draft: identify the object in the first representation, follow it into the next, and state the dimensions or explanatory scope that changed. Consider shared position, local naming, retained quantities, or a justified transition cue while preserving necessary qualifications. Record visible correspondence, native positioning, and independent reader understanding separately. Professional examples support reasoning, not claims of faster Chinese-slide reading or universally superior techniques. Use existing Art records and the bounded reader workflow; add no template, checklist, or scoring gate.

## Make direction refer to a stated quantity

Identify the quantity and its reference before encoding direction. An amount with its source sign, a change in that item, the arithmetic effect of that change on a named result, and an evaluation of business performance are different meanings. A decrease in an expense can be negative in an item-change table and positive in an explanation of profit growth. Verify the operation and ownership before selecting which quantity to display. Arithmetic reconciliation does not establish an economic cause or persistence.

Consider what a graphic form invites the viewer to read. Lines can invite continuous-change readings, while separate bars or points can invite discrete comparisons. An arrow may suggest sequence, change, influence, or causality. Interpretation depends on content and task; neither a fixed meaning nor a ban on lines follows from the shape. Direct numbers, necessary names, operations, position, and shared references can cooperate to convey the intended relation.

When adjacent representations give the same result different signs or directions, trace the actual draft for the entity, period, unit, and current reference. Is the viewer reading the item, its change, or its effect on a named result? Retained original amounts, an explicit subtraction, or a verified difference can help as appropriate; a standard note on every page is unnecessary. A minus sign, bar direction, and color need not simultaneously mean a decrease, a negative contribution, and worse performance.

Read the actual page and its neighbors for a recognizable quantity and reference. Keep numerical checks, interpretation of graphic form, and reader understanding as distinct evidence. One correct restatement does not establish faster reading, and task-specific research does not prescribe a universal chart ranking. Use existing Art records and bounded repair allowances without a new symbol checklist, color rule, score, or page quota.

## Give attention a useful hierarchy

Use size, position, contrast, spacing, area and alignment together to guide the
reader. Main evidence should receive the attention needed to understand the
claim; necessary conditions remain readable and attached to what they qualify.
Equal editorial levels may have different visual weights when their roles differ.

Look at what actually attracts the eye. A border, icon or empty container can
dominate even when its text is minor. Reallocate the space or emphasis when the
visible hierarchy misrepresents the information. Whitespace can separate ideas,
support a focal point or give the eye a pause. Judge those effects on the actual
page, including whether unused space could relieve cramped information.

## Let the whole composition communicate

Treat the page as a visual explanation. Decide how the overall arrangement
can make relationships, emphasis and reading order perceptible alongside the
words. Typography is one expressive resource among space, scale, position,
shape, imagery, grouping, contrast, depth, rhythm and deliberate absence.
Choose freely from those and other appropriate means for this particular
content; the list is illustrative, not a required toolkit or finite menu.

A shared enclosure might explain scope; contrasting regions might distinguish
main material from support; open space or an image might establish the useful
focus instead. Use a treatment only when its visible effect helps the reader.
Frames, blocks and background depth are possibilities, not mandatory objects.
Do not infer a box for every heading, a highlighted panel for every page, or a
repeating emphasis recipe from these examples.

Inspect the whole page before reading each sentence: what relationship or
priority does its arrangement communicate? Then read the actual content and
check that the two agree. If important distinctions exist only in bold or
colored words, reconsider the composition where a spatial or pictorial
expression would help. Some pages benefit from restrained typographic design;
judge the result rather than requiring non-text decoration.

Balance work must preserve useful expression. Making every surface pale or
removing every frame can erase hierarchy, just as indiscriminate heavy fills
can distort it. Reallocate visible weight as needed, inspect the page and its
neighbors, and retain the model's freedom to find a better composition. No
prescribed layout, object count or aesthetic score replaces this judgment.

## Balance the weight that is actually visible

Decide where this page's visual weight should sit from the main judgment and
the relationships the reader needs to follow. Purposeful asymmetry can give a
main claim room and keep supporting conditions close. Let the actual result
determine whether the eye finds a useful focal point and can move comfortably
through the page.

Judge color together with geometry and text. A large, saturated fill can pull
more attention than a more important passage in a plain region. Luminance and
color contrast, filled area, position, clustered objects, borders and text
density all contribute to the weight a reader perceives. A dark, dense text
column can also outweigh a larger pale region. Matching widths, heading levels
or numbers of objects does not establish a balanced page.

Use brand color according to its visible effect. A full panel may identify a
primary relationship, but applying it to a long supporting paragraph can make
that paragraph appear to be the main claim. Consider a smaller accent, lighter
surface, narrower emphasis or a different allocation of space when those
choices preserve the intended hierarchy. Judge text contrast and reading
comfort again after changing the color treatment.

Inspect the color thumbnail before relying on the wording: where does the eye
land, what pulls it next, and does a secondary colored region overpower the
main evidence? Then read the full-size draft to check whether dense text,
necessary conditions or isolated content change that impression. Grayscale
can help diagnose a particular contrast problem, but retain the color view
as evidence of what readers will actually see.

Revise the cause of an imbalance. Adjust the extent or intensity of the fill,
position, grouping, typography or available space as the content requires.
Unused space should support separation, focus or a pause; an empty side alone
does not counter the pull of a heavy colored block. Reinspect the changed page
and its neighbors for accidental shifts of emphasis and repeated heavy areas.
Record the specific attention problem and the observed effect of the revision
in existing Art work notes. Color proportions, geometric symmetry and automatic
balance scores are not substitutes for this judgment.

## Compose a sequence the reader can sustain

Inspect the actual sequence as well as each page. Repeated placement can help
comparison and orientation. Check that the comparison dimensions stay coherent,
the new evidence receives attention and the reader benefits from the shared
structure. A claim that repetition "supports comparison" needs to be visible
in those pages.

Visual fatigue can arise when evidence, explanation, process and conclusion all
require the same scanning effort and receive the same emphasis. Consider the
combined effect of silhouette, density, focal point, medium and reading path.
Each page can have a plausible local explanation while the sequence still feels
monotonous. Art must notice and revise that experience.

Retain the story and useful continuity. Change composition, emphasis, grouping
or pacing where they improve understanding. A different color, icon or random
layout rotation does not establish a different reading experience. There is no
fixed repetition limit, prescribed number of layouts or required contrast page.

Shared drawing code may handle text measurement and native objects. Its default
coordinates, column widths or container count must not become the design brief.
Decide the composition first and implement the chosen design with suitable tools.

## Inspect, revise and observe the result

Use thumbnails to judge the first impression, focal points and the sequence.
Read full-size pages to judge relationships, conditions, density and reading
comfort. Compare the intended experience with the actual experience: what is
noticed first, what remains hard to connect, and where the eye or reader tires.
Seeing all the text or staying inside containers proves only part of the work.

When a problem is visible, name its concrete effect and revise the design that
causes it. Then inspect the changed page and its place in the sequence again.
Use existing work notes to record material observations, actual changes and
their observed effects. Retained issues need specific reasons; stock statements
about tidy boxes or comparable sections do not demonstrate improvement.

Art performs this judgment before locking the baseline. Output implements it;
audit does not supply missing design decisions. Preserve the existing handoff,
native editing boundaries and responsibility for wording changes. Do not add
an aesthetic score, extra approval form or automatic layout quota.

## Illustrative ways to think

These hypothetical page tasks illustrate reasoning, not reusable layouts:

- A payment undertaking: help readers see the participants, movement of documents
  and payment conditions. Place an accompanying fee explanation where its role
  is clear, without turning it into an invented final process step.
- A safety review: make the supported relationship among incoming reports,
  evaluation and possible changes to use conditions apparent. Preserve the
  distinction between a reported event and evidence of causation.
- A performance comparison: make a supported difference visible alongside its
  period, unit and scope. Let the reader inspect the evidence behind the claim
  without assembling it from unrelated paragraph boxes.

Choose an original composition for the actual content. The measure of these
ideas is whether the reader can see and understand that content more readily.

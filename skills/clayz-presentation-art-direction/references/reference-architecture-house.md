# Reference-architecture house

Use this protocol for capability houses, enterprise architecture overviews, data or AI platform maps, and operating-system diagrams. It is a page archetype under `strategy-deployment`; it is not a fifth material route.

## Research before drawing

When the user requests broad benchmarking, build the requested corpus before composing. If the request specifies 50–100 sources, count 50–100 distinct diagram-bearing documents or pages rather than treating one gallery index as many sources.

1. Prefer primary official architecture centers, deployable reference architectures, design guides, and technical documentation. Use secondary galleries only to discover primary material.
2. Sample multiple independent publishers and architecture families: data platforms, analytics, AI or agents, governance, security, operations, and hybrid infrastructure. Record an exception when one vendor dominates the domain.
3. Keep a task-local ledger with title, publisher, URL, access date, architecture purpose, structural grammar, and adoption decision.
4. Extract relationships, not pixels: outcome placement, layer order, cross-cutting controls, boundaries, flow direction, feedback, ownership, and visual hierarchy.
5. Record `never_copy` boundaries for brand identity, master, theme, product icons, wording, media, and coordinates.
6. Cite representative primary sources in speaker notes or the source record. If the resulting method or tool is published, thank the referenced authors and design teams in its public provenance files.

Do not bundle downloaded diagrams or reconstruct a vendor's product stack. The research corpus is evidence for independent synthesis, not a template library.

## House contract

A strong house must make the architecture testable at a glance:

| Structural role | Required meaning | Failure signal |
|---|---|---|
| Roof | business outcome, decision loop, or operating promise | a generic technology title |
| Interior floors | ordered capability transformation from sources to use | equal noun bands with no dependency |
| Pillars | controls that genuinely cross multiple floors | governance mentioned once as a decorative label |
| Foundation | infrastructure and operational substrate | cloud products presented as the main story |
| Upward path | how data becomes meaning, evidence, judgment, and action | arrows that imply causality without a mechanism |
| Return path | how outcomes, corrections, and exceptions revise the system | no feedback, versioning, approval, or rollback |
| Responsibility boundary | what models may propose and what people must decide | responsibility silently delegated to AI |

For ChatBI or enterprise cognition, test four additional roles:

- Put user experiences and actions above the reasoning machinery.
- Give investigation orchestration its own layer: context, hypotheses, tools, counter-evidence, uncertainty, and stopping conditions.
- Treat business semantics as executable reality: objects, relationships, metrics, dimensions, grain, effective time, evidence, provenance, and ownership.
- Make feedback a governed production process: challenge, attribution, expert adjudication, regression, versioned release, effect tracking, and rollback.

## Composition rules

1. Start with a blank or user-approved theme. Never import a research source's master.
2. Give the roof, floors, pillars, and foundation distinct visual roles. Do not simulate strength with thicker borders alone.
3. Use one dominant upward reading path and one explicit return path. Route arrows before placing rooms so connectors remain legible.
4. Limit sub-capabilities to the few that prove each layer's responsibility. Move implementation detail to notes or follow-on pages.
5. Use native editable shapes unless a user-approved asset has a necessary semantic role and traceable rights.
6. Preserve a management reading order: outcome first, transformation second, controls third, implementation last.
7. Render at full slide size and reject clipped labels, pillar collisions, unreadable microtext, connector ambiguity, and decorative whitespace.

## Rejection tests

Reject the page when any of the following is true:

- It could describe any company after replacing the title.
- A viewer cannot explain why one floor sits above another.
- Governance, security, quality, or observability do not visibly constrain the affected layers.
- The feedback path ends in “retrain” without evidence attribution, human adjudication, versioning, and rollback.
- Product names outnumber business capabilities.
- The diagram hides uncertainty or makes the model appear responsible for organizational decisions.
- The result resembles one reviewed vendor diagram more than it expresses the current problem.

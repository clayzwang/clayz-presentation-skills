# Asset and Template Grammar

Templates, icons, chart patterns, table patterns, and sample decks are a repertoire of moves, not finished answers. Art Direction may learn what a candidate makes possible, then must re-derive the page from current Logic, Copy, material route, and semantic relations.

## Template candidates

A template skeleton is admissible only when it fits the page job, relation type, content load, dominant medium, and neighbor rhythm. Record the adopted principle in `use_for` and the prohibited imitation in `do_not_copy`.

Never inherit exact wording, coordinates, master/layout identity, brand styling, proprietary media, or arbitrary fill ratios. If the current content cannot explain why the skeleton works, reject it.

## Icon grammar

Use an icon only when it encodes an object, action, state, channel, or distinction that would otherwise take longer to recognize. It must not decorate a heading that is already clear.

Prefer one coherent icon family per semantic system. Mixing families is allowed only when the difference itself carries meaning and is recorded as an intentional mix. Brand marks are not generic icons; use them only with verified rights and brand rules.

Prefer editable SVG for generic interface and business symbols. Do not convert a bitmap or trademark into an SVG merely to make it editable.

## Governed selection

Every slide records an `asset_strategy`:

- `template_mode=derive-not-clone`;
- semantic roles required from assets;
- candidate and selected registry IDs;
- selection rationale and family-consistency decision;
- one license record for every selected asset; and
- explicit `never_copy` boundaries.

Selected IDs must be a subset of reviewed candidates. A selected external asset without source and license evidence is a validation failure. No selection is also a valid decision when typography, native shapes, a chart, or a table expresses the meaning better.

This contract improves flexibility: it constrains accountability, not style. It does not prescribe a fixed icon count, template category, grid fill ratio, or visual score.
